SIMPLE (INDEFINITE)TENSES
Present Simple
- регулярно повторяющееся действие, общеизвестный факт
Сигнальные слова: every day/ week/ month/ year, usually, always, often, seldom, really
Образование: V; Vs (3л. Ед. ч.)
+ I work. He works.
? Do I work? Does he work?
- I don’t work. He doesn’t work.
Past  Simple
- Однократное действие в прошлом
Сигнальные слова: yesterday, last week/ month/ year, … ago
Образование: Ved (правильные глаголы), V2 (неправильные глаголы)
+ I worked. He came.
? Did I work? Did he come?
- I didn’t work. He didn’t come.
Future Simple
- Однократное действие в будущем
Сигнальные слова: tomorrow, next week/ month/ year, in two days
Образование: will + V
+ I will work.
? Will I work?
- I will not (won’t) work.

US vs* UK Universities: Key Differences, Admissions, and How to Choose
Choosing between the US and UK university systems is one of the most important decisions a student can make. Both systems include highly respected institutions and can lead you to succeed in your studies and career, but they differ meaningfully in structure, admissions, cost, teaching style, student experience, and long-term career pathways. For this reason, the question is rarely about which system is “better”, but which is better organized for a particular student’s academic profile, interests, learning style, and future plans.
Because of these differences there is no single "best" choice. The right choice depends on you: your interests, how you like to learn, what subjects you want to study, and what you want to do in the future.
To make a good decision, you need to think about more than just university rankings or how famous a university is. You should also consider:
which programs match your academic interests;
how flexible the program is;
how much money you will need;
what career you want;
where you want to live and work.
When you understand these differences clearly, you can make a better choice about where to study.
A clear understanding of these differences is essential to make a better choice about where to study.

Teaching and Assessment Differences Between UK and US Universities
One of the important differences between universities in the UK and the US is how students are taught and tested. The way students are assessed can vary a lot depending on the school and the subject.
In the US students are often assessed through different types of work, such as essays, problem sets, projects, presentations, participation in class, and exams throughout the semester. This means that students get regular feedback and have many chances to show what they know. This system can be good for students who like to have ongoing support and check their progress regularly.
In the UK, students usually have more exams at the end of the term or year, especially in traditional subjects. However, this doesn’t mean there are no other forms of assessment. Many courses also include coursework, lab reports, portfolios, and practical work, depending on the
subject. Generally, UK students might need to work more independently between assessments and do well on fewer, more important exams.
When comparing UK and US education systems, students should think about which style suits them best. Some students do better with regular structure and feedback, while others prefer to study more independently and dive deeper into their subjects.
UK vs US Admissions
The way students apply to universities in the UK and the US is quite different.
In the US university admissions look at the whole student. This means that while good grades are important, universities also consider other things. These can include essays, activities outside of school (like sports or clubs), recommendation letters from teachers, and other signs of a student’s initiative and fit for the university. They want to understand the student as a complete person, not just based on exam results.
In the UK university admissions focus more on academics and specific courses. Students apply through a system called UCAS for certain degree programs. Decisions are mostly based on how well students have done in school, their predicted grades, and if they fit well the course they want to study. The UCAS application now includes three specific questions to answer instead of just one personal statement. This change helps students reflect more clearly on their reasons for choosing their course.
For some competitive courses in the UK, students may need to take extra tests depending on the subject and university. These tests can include the LNAT, UCAT, TSA, TMUA, or ESAT. Interviews are also important for some universities, especially at Oxford and Cambridge or for courses like Medicine. These interviews focus on how students think and solve problems with new information.
In the US interviews are usually more personal. They help universities get a better idea of who the student is. It's important for students to prepare well for interviews in both countries because the style and purpose of the interviews can be very different.
For students applying to UK universities it’s important to show a deep understanding of their chosen subject, a real interest in it, and clear reasons for why they want to study that course.
